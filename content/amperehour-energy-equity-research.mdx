## The view in one paragraph

AmpereHour Energy is a credible early mover in Indian grid batteries, a market that is moving from tenders to actual construction. It nearly quadrupled revenue in FY25 and still made a profit, which is rare for a company scaling this fast. My view is **positive but conditional**: the company is unlisted, so there is no rating or price target, and my indicative enterprise value of **₹400 to ₹1,000 Cr** (base case about ₹772 Cr) depends on assumptions that need to be checked against its FY26 filings.

## What the company does

AmpereHour (AmpereHour Solar Technology Pvt. Ltd., founded in Pune in 2017) builds **battery energy storage systems (BESS)**: large battery installations that store electricity when it is cheap or plentiful, such as solar power at noon, and release it when it is needed, such as in the evening. It designs, integrates, controls and maintains these systems, and owns its energy management software, **ELINA**.

It started as a software and controls business, became a project integrator, and in July 2026 opened a manufacturing and integration facility at Chakan, Pune, with a planned capacity of 5 GWh. Its biggest project so far is a 180 MW / 360 MWh system in Gujarat for IndiGrid, commissioned in May 2026.

## Why it could work

1. **Huge demand that has barely started.** India's power regulator (CEA) tracked 143.82 GWh of battery projects in May 2026, but only 7.50 GWh (5.2%) was actually running. The official need by 2031–32 is 236.22 GWh.
2. **Proven step-up in project size.** The company went from a 40 MWh project in Delhi to a 360 MWh project in Gujarat, nine times bigger by energy stored.
3. **More control than a pure integrator.** Its own factory, its own software (ELINA) and a digital factory-testing suite give it more say over cost, quality and delivery.
4. **Profitable growth.** Revenue rose from ₹21.8 Cr to ₹83.9 Cr in one year with a 6.9% profit margin. My base case assumes the EBITDA margin rises from 7.2% in FY25 to 12.5% by FY30.

## How I valued it

I forecast revenue as **MWh delivered × price per MWh**, growing from ₹83.9 Cr in FY25 to ₹1,242 Cr in FY30E in the base case. I then used a **discounted cash flow (DCF)** model: estimate the cash the business will generate in future years and convert it into today's money at a 15% discount rate.

Cash flow is negative until FY29E, because a fast-growing project business has to buy batteries and equipment before customers pay. So most of the value comes from the end-point, which I set at 10 times FY30E EBITDA. That gives an **enterprise value of about ₹772 Cr**.

The wide spread between the bear and bull cases is the honest answer: the value depends mostly on future margins and the exit multiple. Cross-checking with revenue multiples (1.0 to 3.5 times FY27E revenue) gives ₹480 to ₹1,680 Cr, which is why I settle on an indicative range of ₹400 to ₹1,000 Cr rather than a single number.

## How it compares

AmpereHour's gross margin is not disclosed." />

AmpereHour grows much faster than its peers, but its 6.9% net margin is below Pace Digitek (11.4%) and Sungrow (15.1%), and it is a fraction of their size. Fluence, a global integrator with a similar software-plus-integration model, earned only a 5.9% adjusted gross margin in its latest quarter. To earn more, AmpereHour has to move toward making its own hardware and charging for its software.

## What worries me

- **Margin squeeze.** Average storage tariffs fell about 52.8% in two years (2024 to 2026), while battery prices rebounded to $70–75 per kWh. Contractors who bid low can lose money when they buy batteries a year later.
- **Small capital base.** About $7.45 million raised in total, against a 5 GWh factory and large projects that tie up cash.
- **Factory utilisation.** A new 5 GWh plant needs steady orders to cover its fixed costs, and Pace Digitek already has 5 GWh installed.
- **Unproven software moat.** No one has yet shown that ELINA earns a software-like margin or raises customers' returns.
- **Limited disclosure.** FY26 revenue, gross margin, order book and debt are not public yet.

Overall risk level: **medium to high**. The market is strong, but the business is capital intensive and prices are under pressure.

## What would change my mind

**More positive if:** FY26 gross margin is above 15%, factory output ramps up, IndiGrid and others place repeat orders, receivable days stay stable, and ELINA revenue is disclosed and growing.

**More negative if:** projects are delayed or written down, margins fall below 5%, the company raises money at a low valuation, or no software revenue shows up.

## Terms used on this page

- **BESS:** battery energy storage system, a large battery that stores grid electricity.
- **MW vs MWh:** MW is how fast power can flow out; MWh is how much energy is stored. A 180 MW / 360 MWh system can run at full power for two hours.
- **Revenue, EBITDA and PAT:** revenue is total sales; EBITDA is profit from operations before interest, tax and depreciation; PAT is the profit left after everything, including tax.
- **Enterprise value (EV):** what the whole business is worth to all its funders, before taking out debt and adding back cash.
- **DCF:** a valuation that adds up future cash flows, shrunk back to today's value because money later is worth less than money now.
- **Exit multiple:** an assumed price for the business at the end of the forecast, here 10 times its FY30E EBITDA.
